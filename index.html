Brute Flies

A fly decides when to turn. You only get to argue with it.

Yaw torque. Every spike is a turn the fly started on its own; nobody pressed anything.

Player 1: A D turn, W S climb and dive, Space lunge
Player 2: arrow keys turn, climb and dive, Enter lunge
How to play

Two flies share a round, featureless arena and fly in all three dimensions. Steer with turn, climb and dive, and lunge to ram the other fly. A lunge that connects does damage and knocks the victim back; being knocked into the wall hurts more. Knock the other fly out, or have more health when the 45 seconds run out. First to three rounds takes the match.

Your steering is only a bias. Each fly keeps making sharp turns of its own, in bursts and long quiet glides, and you cannot switch that off. A fly in the middle of one of its own turns is hard to read, so hits on it do half damage.

After every round each fly molts twice, and you choose neither. The habit molt follows what the fly did on its own that round: many turns grow Agility, a long glide grows Speed, wide turns grow Whim. The leap molt picks any trait and a heavy-tailed size: usually +1, now and then +6.

Speed is cruising pace. Agility is how fast you can steer. Brawn is ram damage. Shell is health. Nerve shortens the wait between lunges. Whim widens the fly's own turns.

The science behind it

The game is based on Maye, Hsieh, Sugihara and Brembs, "Order in Spontaneous Behavior", PLoS ONE 2(5): e443 (2007). They tethered fruit flies in a uniformly white arena with nothing to react to, and recorded the turning force each fly produced. The flies kept making turning manoeuvres anyway.

The gaps between those turns were not what a random process such as coin-flipping would give, and they were not regular either. They had a heavy tail, with many short gaps and a few very long ones, in the manner of a Lévy flight, and carried the mathematical signature of a nonlinear system. The authors argue that brains contain a mechanism that initiates behaviour on its own account, somewhere between chance and necessity.

In this game each fly carries a toy version of such an initiator: a noisy chaotic map sets the gap to the next turn, its direction and its size. It is an illustration of the idea, not the model from the paper.
